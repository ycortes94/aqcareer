During my time working predominantly with low-income, first-generation Americans, one recurring theme in our sessions was the struggle my clients faced when confronting risk. Whether they leaned towards risk avoidance or were simply risk-averse, managing risk was a topic that frequently emerged in our sessions. For many of these individuals, there was a lack of role models in their lives who had successfully navigated risk and seen their efforts pay off.

Here are some practical recommendations, drawn from best practices for risk-taking, that I've found valuable in helping my clients embrace risk with confidence while mitigating fear:



  1. Assess the Situation: Before diving into any risky venture, it's crucial to thoroughly understand the potential outcomes and consequences of our actions. Taking time to weigh the pros and cons ensures that decisions are made thoughtfully. I often encourage clients to create a simple pro-con list as a starting point for assessing a situation.

  2. Set Clear Goals: Clearly defining objectives and understanding the purpose behind taking a specific risk is essential. Having clear goals provides direction and keeps individuals focused and motivated. Personally, I find that visual aids such as mind maps or vision boards are effective tools for helping clients translate their goals into tangible manifestations.

  3. Manage Resources: Evaluating the resources at our disposal, whether it's time, money, or skills, is the next crucial step. Many clients need support in this area, as they may struggle to recognize and utilize their own strengths and resources effectively. Proper resource management is key to minimizing the likelihood of failure and reducing losses.

  4. Be Prepared for Failure: It's important to acknowledge that not all risks lead to failure, but also that failure is a natural part of the process. Normalizing the possibility of failure and reframing it as an opportunity to learn and pivot is crucial. Encouraging clients to reflect on their failures helps them assess their behaviors and refine their approach for future decision-making.

  5. Seek Feedback: Encouraging clients to leverage their network and community as a resource allows them to gather valuable input from others with relevant experience or expertise. Constructive feedback can help identify blind spots and refine approaches, increasing the likelihood of success.


Taking calculated risks is not just a skill but a mindset that can greatly impact our client’s personal and professional growth. By embracing risk with careful consideration and strategic planning, individuals can unlock new opportunities and achieve their goals. As practitioners, it's essential to continue empowering our clients to navigate risk effectively. I invite fellow practitioners to share their strategies for coaching clients to mitigate risk, as collective wisdom and diverse perspectives further enrich our approach and support our clients in their journey towards success.
