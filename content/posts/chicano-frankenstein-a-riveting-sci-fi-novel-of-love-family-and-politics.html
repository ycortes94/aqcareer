Author Daniel Olivas writes a powerful tale that illustrates profound life truths through a fantastical United States of America that has mastered prolonging life after death. Through this unapologetically Chicanx love story and a reimagining of Mary Shelley's classic "Frankenstein," "Chicano Frankenstein" narrates the story of an unnamed man as he navigates work and love. Yet, Olivas masterfully delves into tough topics. He considers the personal impacts and broader implications of scientific evolution. He also examines the role of the American government's regulation and oversight over reanimated individuals intended to fill a gap in the American workforce. Olivas nudges us to remember the truth: politics is deeply personal.


"Chicano Frankenstein" simultaneously explores the dynamics of capitalism and politics. Much like in the classic "Frankenstein," it poses the question, “Who is the Monster?” but this time, it holds a mirror up to society, the reader, capitalism, and the government. Through this reflection, Olivas encourages a critical examination of the sources of monstrosity in our modern world. In this novel, much like our own experiences in this country over the past decade, the government crafts harmful and polarizing narratives that cause national divides, spread hate, and deeply harm individuals on a personal level. Olivas highlights how the government betrays its constituents by forming damaging alliances with various industries, exacerbating social fractures, and undermining public trust.


Finally, "Chicano Frankenstein’s" tale of a reanimated community serving the workforce needs serves as an allegory for our current work conditions, shedding light on the stories of an increasingly disenfranchised workforce. This workforce, often referred to as alien and dehumanized, is valued for its labor but not recognized for its humanity. Olivas critiques this exploitation, highlighting how society, in the name of capitalism, benefits from their efforts while denying their intrinsic worth and rights.

Olivas explores the implications of being “wiped clean,” a state where one loses family, history, and culture. He considers the personal conflicts and internal struggles that emerge when people are denied access to their personal histories. In this state, reanimated people don’t belong to any community, have no memories, or familial roots and navigate the world without the capacity to know themselves. Through this exploration, Olivas highlights the critical role of memory and heritage in shaping our identities and sense of belonging.


As a Career Development professional, I always find myself connecting everything to career growth and professional fulfillment. The novel "Chicano Frankenstein" offers a profound commentary on the modern workforce. The reanimated workforce serves as a stark metaphor for contemporary labor issues, highlighting the importance of recognizing and valuing the humanity of workers. As we navigate the world of work, it is crucial to recognize that our work and professional environments are deeply intertwined with our identities, histories, and personal values.


It's safe to say that "Chicano Frankenstein" is now one of my favorite books! I highly recommend this text for use in a variety of learning spaces, given its ability to navigate sensitive topics in an allegorical and engaging manner. Olivas masterfully addresses complex issues such as identity, government, capitalism, and humanity through a compelling narrative. Read this book and reflect on its profound insights!
